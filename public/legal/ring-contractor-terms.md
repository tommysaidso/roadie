## 1. THE RELATIONSHIP

**This is a film production.** ROADIE: The Ring is a documentary, and the band is
engaged as a **paid performer on that production** — not as a support act on a
promoter's show. That distinction is the whole structure: the engagement,
the paperwork and the rules we follow are film production rules, because that is
what this is.

The band is hired as an **independent contractor** by **the Producer** for
a specified ring (route, dates, number of stops). This is not employment. The
band is not a partner, co-producer, or co-owner of the production. The Producer
is the general contractor. The ring is the Producer's production. The band is talent on it.

- **Non-union.** This production is not signatory to a performer or crew union
  agreement. If you are a member of one, check your own obligations before you
  accept — that is yours to know, and we would rather you raised it now.
- **Local hire.** Crew are engaged locally at each stop wherever the town can
  supply them, on the same film-production basis. It is cheaper, it is better —
  the person who knows the back roads lives there — and it is the point of
  routing through these towns at all.
- **We pay the trip.** Travel, accommodation and per diem for the touring party
  are production costs carried by the Producer, on top of the performance fee. You are
  not funding your own road costs out of the guarantee.

### Who we take, and who we do not

The ring is a small production with a direct relationship to the act. Two
things put an act out of scope, and neither is a judgement on the music:

- **Agented acts.** If you are represented by a booking agent for live work, we
  are not the right fit. The economics here assume no agent layer, and we are
  not going to negotiate a production this size through one.
- **Conflicts with the platform.** An existing deal that conflicts with the
  licences in this agreement — an exclusive that would bar the sync licence, or
  a commitment that competes with the platform itself. Tell us up front; it is
  far better found now than after a date is booked.

If either applies you can still be on the platform. It is the ring that does not
fit, not you.

### Where rings run

**Rings do not cross the border.** A Canadian ring is crewed, billed and paid in
Canada; a US ring in the USA. Acts are not moved across for dates, so artist
visa classes do not arise.

In the USA, engagement is **film production crew on a work-for-hire basis** —
the ring is a documentary shoot and that is the framework the production runs
on. Paperwork follows that: W-9 before first payment, 1099-NEC at year end.

## 2. COMPENSATION

- **Guarantee:** $3,000 CAD per show, escrowed by the Producer before the ring begins. Non-refundable. Paid regardless of door, weather, or attendance.
- **The guarantee is per SHOW, per ACT — not per head.** A five-piece and a solo
  artist booked for the same slot are paid the same $3,000. You are being paid
  to perform that night, not by the headcount you bring. If one person can hold
  a crowd the way five can, that is worth exactly the same to this production.
  (Per-person costs — meals, per diem, rooms — are covered per touring member on
  top of the guarantee, and those genuinely do scale with the size of the act.)
- **Door share:** 60% of door revenue per stop, paid on settlement night-of.
- **Incidentals covered by the Producer:** fuel/transport, accommodation, meals/per diem for all touring band members for the duration of the ring. The band does not pay road costs.
- **No deductions.** The guarantee and door share are paid gross. The band handles their own tax obligations as independent contractors. The Producer issues the year-end slip for its own jurisdiction — **T4A** in Canada, **1099-NEC** in the United States (payments of $600 USD or more, against a Form W-9 supplied before first payment).

## 3. MUSIC RIGHTS + PLATFORM

### What the band keeps:
- **Ownership of their songs.** Neither the Producer nor UARX acquires publishing, master rights, or copyright in the band's existing catalogue. The music is theirs.
- **Performance royalties.** All SOCAN/PRO performance royalties from live shows belong to the band as they always would.

### What the band grants the Producer:
- **mymymy platform presence.** The band agrees to have a profile on mymymy (roadie.mymymy.studio) for the duration of the ring and 12 months following. Profile includes bio, photos, music links, show dates. The band can maintain their profile beyond 12 months at their discretion.
- **Sync licence for production use.** The band grants the Producer a non-exclusive sync licence for their original music performed during the ring, for use in:
  - The ROADIE: The Ring docu-series (all episodes, all platforms, in perpetuity)
  - Music videos produced from ring footage (published via mymymy, band retains right to use)
  - Promotional materials for ROADIE and mymymy platforms
- **NOT included in the sync licence:** use of the band's music in **advertiser commercials**. If the band's music is used in a commercial produced for the anchor advertiser, that is a **separate sync deal** negotiated and compensated independently. This will never be assumed or buried.

## 4. FILMING + LIKENESS

The band consents to being filmed, recorded, and photographed during:
- All performances on the ring
- Behind-the-scenes, travel, and road footage
- Interviews and on-camera conversations (participation is voluntary per instance — the band can decline any specific interview but cannot decline general performance filming)

### Likeness usage:
- **Docu-series:** unlimited, all platforms, in perpetuity. The band appears as themselves in a documentary. No additional compensation beyond the ring guarantee.
- **Music videos:** produced from ring footage, published via mymymy. Band receives copies and retains the right to use for their own promotion.
- **Promotional materials:** ROADIE/mymymy platform promotion. Band's name, likeness, and performance footage.
- **Advertiser commercials:** if the band is **identifiable** (not background/atmosphere) in a commercial produced for the anchor advertiser, a **one-time national buyout** is negotiated separately. This is never included in the ring guarantee. The buyout amount is disclosed and agreed before the commercial is produced.

## 5. NO RIDER POLICY

**There is no technical rider. There is no hospitality rider.**

The Producer provides:
- Professional stage (container stage, 20'×16', full PA, lighting, monitors)
- Sound (line array PA, 4 monitor wedges, full mic kit, DI boxes, digital mixer)
- Lights (DMX-controlled LED rig, AI-reactive or operator-run)
- Power (60kW generator, self-contained)
- Accommodation for all touring band members (booked and paid by the Producer)
- Meals / per diem for all touring band members
- Transport coordination (band provides own vehicle or the Producer arranges)

The band provides:
- Their own backline (instruments, amps, pedals, drumkit if required)
- Their own in-ear monitors if preferred (wedges provided otherwise)
- Their talent and professionalism

**What the band does NOT get to request:**
- Dressing room specifications
- Hospitality amenities beyond meals/per diem
- Soundcheck time beyond the allocated window (typically 30–45 min)
- Set length changes outside the agreed performance window
- Guest list beyond a reasonable number (2–4 per band member)
- Merchandise table — **provided by the Producer at no charge**, band keeps 100% of merch sales at the table

**The stage is what it is.** The container deploys the same rig at every stop. The band plays on what's provided. This is a touring production with a consistent technical spec, not a one-off headline booking. The consistency is the point — every audience at every stop gets the same professional experience.

## 6. CONDUCT + CANCELLATION

- The band agrees to conduct themselves professionally at all times on the ring. This includes interactions with audiences, venue hosts, sponsors, crew, and community members.
- **No alcohol or drugs on stage or in the container.** Personal time is personal, but the production is dry. This aligns with the ring's dry-preferred model.
- The Producer may terminate the contractor agreement immediately for:
  - No-show without advance notice
  - On-stage conduct that endangers the audience, crew, or production
  - Violation of the no-substances-on-production policy
  - Damage to production equipment
- If the Producer terminates for cause, the band forfeits remaining guarantee payments for uncompleted shows. Guarantee for completed shows is still owed.
- If the band needs to leave the ring for emergency reasons, the Producer will work to accommodate and will pay for completed shows. The band is not penalized for genuine emergencies.

## 7. LOCAL ARTISTS (non-ring)

Local artists invited to play an opening slot at a specific stop are NOT ring contractors. They receive:
- Stage access for their set (typically 20–30 minutes)
- The experience and exposure of playing a professional stage in their own town
- Filming consent applies (same as ring band — they will be on camera)
- **No guarantee, no door share, no incidentals coverage**
- They may be offered a nominal appearance fee at the Producer's discretion
- If a local artist travels to a subsequent stop at their own expense, they may be invited to play again but do not become a ring contractor

## 9. INDEMNITY, INSURANCE + LIABILITY

**This runs both ways. Each side carries its own conduct.**

### The band indemnifies the Producer for:
- **Their music.** The band warrants they own or control the rights to every
  song they perform and that performing it does not infringe anyone else's
  copyright. If a third party claims otherwise, that claim is the band's to
  answer and the band covers the Producer's costs in it.
- **Their gear.** Backline, amps, instruments and any equipment the band brings.
  If their rig injures someone or damages property, that is theirs.
- **Their conduct.** Acts or omissions by the band or any member — on stage, on
  site, or in accommodation booked for them.
- **Their tax and employment obligations.** The band is engaged as an
  independent contractor and is responsible for its own remittances. If a
  taxing authority comes to the Producer because the band did not file, the band covers
  it.

### The Producer indemnifies the band for:
- **The production.** The stage, PA, lighting, rigging, power and structures are
  the Producer's. If production equipment or production negligence causes injury or
  damage, that is the Producer's to answer.
- **The site.** Permits, occupancy, and site safety at each stop.
- **The footage.** Use of the docu-series and promotional material within the
  licences granted here.

### Insurance
- The Producer carries commercial general liability covering the production, the
  structures, and the site.
- The band carries, or is covered under production for, workers' compensation as
  required where the show is (provincial WCB in Canada, state scheme in the USA).
- The band is responsible for insuring its own instruments and gear. Production
  insurance does not cover a band's backline.
- Either side provides a certificate of insurance on request.

### Limits
- Neither side is liable to the other for indirect or consequential loss — lost
  profits, lost future bookings, reputational loss.
- The Producer's total liability to the band on a ring is limited to the total
  guarantee payable to that band for that ring. This does not limit liability
  for death, personal injury, or anything else that cannot be limited by law.
- Nothing here limits either party's liability for gross negligence, wilful
  misconduct, or fraud.

### Survival
The indemnities, the music-rights warranty, the licences granted, and the
likeness consents survive the end of the ring. Footage already shot stays
covered.

## 10. CONDUCT ON THE PRODUCTION — NEUTRAL GROUND

**The ring is a music production. It is not a platform for a campaign.**

The Producer takes no political position and does not host one. On the production:

- **The stage is for the music.** Not for political campaigning, partisan
  organising, endorsement of candidates or parties, or divisive advocacy from
  the stage or in production content.
- **We follow the law of the country we are in.** Canadian law in Canada, US
  federal and state law in the USA, and the bylaws of the town hosting the stop.
  Where a local rule and a preference conflict, the local rule wins.
- **Everyone is welcome and everyone is treated the same.** The Producer does not
  discriminate on any ground protected by the BC Human Rights Code, the Canadian
  Human Rights Act, or applicable US federal and state law. This is not a
  political position — it is the law in both countries and it is also simply how
  this production is run.
- **Respect the town.** Every stop is somebody's home. Crew, hosts, volunteers,
  audiences and neighbours get basic courtesy. The production is a guest.
- **Your own views are your own.** What anyone believes, says or posts on their
  own time and their own platforms is their business. This section governs
  conduct on the production, not what a person thinks.

A band's own material is their artistic expression and this is not a content
review of their songs. What it does mean: the production itself stays neutral
ground, and anyone using the stage or the production to campaign — in any
direction — is asked to stop.

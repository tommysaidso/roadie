# ROADIE — the Netflix approach

Draft only. Nothing goes out without Ben's sign-off.

---

## How this one is different

A warm introduction is not a cold email with a friend's name on it. Your friend is
spending their own credibility to forward this, so the note has to do two jobs at once:
**give the executive a reason in the first three lines**, and **make your friend look
good for having sent it**.

That means it must survive being forwarded with nothing but "worth a look" on top of it.
No attachments in the first send — an attachment is a decision, and you want a reply.

**Send your friend two things:** the short forwardable note below, and a separate line
just for them telling them who it should go to. Let them choose the person; they know the
building and you don't.

**Who it should reach:** Unscripted / Documentary Series, at Director or VP level. Canada
originals if that team is separate. Not a coordinator — a coordinator has to sell it
upward, which is the same problem you started with.

---

## The note (forwardable)

Subject: `ROADIE — 8 bands, 8 towns, the ads are made in the episode`

---

Hi [Name],

[Friend] suggested I send this to you directly.

**ROADIE** is a returning docu-series. Eight bands who have never toured each get a circuit
of small towns — the places national tours drive past. One band per route, one route per
episode. They arrive with nothing booked past the room, they have to make the town care,
and at the end of the night the door money is counted on camera in front of everyone.

There is no elimination and no judges. The question every hour asks is whether the town
shows up, and it answers itself on camera. Sometimes it is a full hall and sometimes it is
forty people and a bartender, and both are the show.

Three things that I think make it worth twenty minutes:

**It casts itself.** Every night has a local opener — a band from that town, on a real
stage, often for the first time. One of them gets a route of their own next season. Season
two is cast on camera during season one, so a renewal needs no development cycle and no
casting call.

**It is already routed in two countries.** Eight circuits and forty-eight towns across
British Columbia and Alberta, and three more circuits and twenty stops across Washington,
Oregon and California — real fairgrounds and expo centres, dated. The format localises to
any market with a highway and a touring desert.

**The ad break is content from the episode.** In every town we write and shoot commercials
for the businesses on the main street — one take, their own counter and staff — and they
keep the masters. On an ad tier those spots run in that episode's break, in market. The
show supplies its own inventory. I have not seen another series that can do that, and it is
also why a fully bonded season costs a fraction of the category norm.

I am not looking for development money to find out whether this works. The routes, the
venue and band registry, the booking and settlement platform and the advertising arm are
built and running today, and there is an interactive layer that lets a viewer stop an
episode and ask it questions in the context of the scene playing.

Happy to send the bible and the numbers, or to walk you through it on a call.

## The separate line to your friend

Not for forwarding — this is the ask.

> Thanks for this. Two things that would help: pick whoever you think is the right person
> rather than the most senior one, and if you can add a line about why you thought of it,
> that is worth more than anything in my note. If it is a no, a no is genuinely useful —
> I would rather know fast than be polite about it.

---

## If they reply

**Send, in this order:** the one-sheet first, the deck second, the financing model only if
they ask. Never all three at once — three documents reads as a person who does not know
which one matters.

**Lead the call with the format, not the money.** They are buying a returnable show. The
budget is a reason it is easy to say yes, not a reason to say yes.

**Be ready for the rights question.** A global streamer will ask for worldwide in
perpetuity. That forecloses the local-version business in every other territory, which is
the largest long-term value in this property. It is worth arguing for territory-limited or
windowed rights even at a lower fee — but decide where the line is before the call, not
during it.

**Be ready for the advertising question.** If they want the commercials off the platform,
that is survivable — the ads still get made and the businesses still keep them, they just
do not run inside the episode. Know in advance what that costs you, because it removes a
large part of the independent revenue.

---

## What not to do

- **Do not attach anything to the first note.** An attachment asks for a decision. A note
  asks for a reply.
- **Do not lead with the budget.** Cheap is not a hook at a streamer; cheap is a closing
  argument.
- **Do not oversell the interactive layer up front.** It is a strong second-meeting asset
  and a weak opening one — it reads as tech, and they are buying a show.
- **Do not name-drop your friend beyond one line.** "[Friend] suggested I send this to you
  directly" is enough. More than that makes it their pitch instead of yours.
- **Do not chase.** One follow-up after two weeks, then leave it. A warm introduction you
  chase becomes a favour you spent.

# ROADIE — the approach

Draft only. Nothing goes out without Ben's sign-off.

---

## Who to send it to

**Send to the brand, not the broadcaster.** In order of who actually says yes:

1. **VP Marketing / CMO, Canada** — the person with a national budget and a mandate to show up in regional markets. Best target.
2. **Director of Brand or Brand Partnerships** — often the one who actually goes looking for something like this, and who will carry it upward for you.
3. **Agency of record, Managing Director or Head of Strategy** — good if the brand outsources everything. Slower, and they may see you as competition for the production fee.
4. **Head of Dealer / Franchise Marketing** — the sleeper. They own co-op advertising funds, they are permanently short of local creative, and forty-eight produced spots solves a problem they already have. Often the easiest first yes in the building.

On LinkedIn, filter by title plus "Canada" and check they've posted about regional, rural or community marketing in the last year. If they have, lead with that in the first line.

**Do not open with the TV show.** Open with the commercials. The show is what makes the commercials unusual — it is not the product you are selling them.

---

## The letter

Subject line, pick one:

- `48 towns, 48 commercials, one brand`
- `A regional campaign that films itself`
- `Your dealers, on camera, in their own markets`

---

Hello [Name],

I run a touring circuit through the small towns of BC and Alberta — eight routes,
forty-eight communities, the places national tours and national campaigns both
drive past.

Here is what I am offering, plainly. We roll into each of those towns with a band
and a camera crew. While we are there, we write and shoot a commercial for a local
business — one take, their counter, their staff, their words. We hand them the
master to keep.

I am looking for one company to take the whole network for two seasons. That gets
you forty-eight finished commercials for your locations in the markets those
locations actually sell into, category exclusivity across every route, and your
name on every night we run. It costs CA$1,000,000 — forty-eight towns at twenty-five thousand
dollars each.

The reason this is worth a conversation and a thirty-second placement is not: at
the end of a normal flight, you have nothing. At the end of this one you own
forty-eight pieces of finished creative, and you have a documented record of having
shown up in forty-eight communities. That is a position competitors cannot buy back
from you later.

There is also a television series being cut from the same footage, which is how we
can do this at that price — the crew is already there. Two seasons, sixteen hours.
Your commercials run inside it, in market.

If it is worth twenty minutes I will walk you through the routes and the numbers.
If the whole network is too big a first step, we do one route for CA$150,000 and you
see it work before you commit to anything else.

UARX MIXEDREALITY CORP
development@evprod.com

## Why they should — the three arguments

Use these if they come back with questions. Not in the first email.

**1. They keep the creative.**
A normal media buy rents attention and leaves them with nothing. This leaves them
with forty-eight finished, owned, in-market commercials at CA$25,000 each —
at or below what a single agency-produced spot costs, and they can run them anywhere,
forever.

**2. Nobody can outbid them on credibility.**
A competitor can outspend them on placements next quarter. A competitor cannot go
back and have been in Lytton and Fort Macleod and Burns Lake in 2026. Showing up is
the one advertising position that cannot be bought retroactively.

**3. The economics only work because we are already going.**
A broadcaster's cost rises with every market added, so their price can only go up.
Ours does not, because the crew, the stage and the routes are already moving. That
is why forty-eight markets prices below what eight would cost anywhere else — and
it is not a discount, it is a structural difference they can verify.

---

## What not to say

- **Do not call it sponsorship.** Sponsorship comes out of a goodwill budget, needs
  a committee, and gets cut first. This is an advertising production buy and it is
  paid from a media budget. That single word choice decides which pocket it comes
  out of.
- **Do not lead with the band, the network, or the interactive layer.** All good,
  none of it is why they write the cheque.
- **Do not apologise for the size of the towns.** The towns are the product.
- **Do not offer a discount in the first exchange.** The micro route at CA$150,000 is
  a smaller first step, not a cheaper version — say it that way.
